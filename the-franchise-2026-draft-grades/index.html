The Method

A projection, not a verdict.

You cannot compute real value over replacement on draft night — that needs a full season of scoring. So these grades run on what we actually know the morning after: what you paid minus what the player is projected to be worth (value vs. market — negative is a bargain, positive is an overpay), and projected points over a replacement-level player at the position, scaled to this room’s settings (ten teams, Half-PPR, two flex spots, which pushes the running back and receiver bar a full starter deeper). Keepers are priced at market this year — keeper cost is the projected auction value at selection, so there’s no discount left to grade. New rule, new math.

Draft night is a head start, not a verdict. Five of our twelve champions had the best draft in the league. The reigning champ had the eighth. We grade the process now and settle the score in January.

One exclusionJosh Jacobs is on the Commissioner’s Exempt List after an offseason arrest, with a hearing that just moved up to Sept. 10. The model behind these numbers was built on projections scraped before any of that, so it still prices him like a healthy $25 workhorse. He gets the kicker treatment: his $2 still counts against Rob’s cap, but he’s excluded from every value and VOR number on this page until there’s something real to grade him on.

Closing Argument
Rob ran the best process in the room. TJ built the best lineup on the field. Janice did neither, twice, on purpose.

Every manager in this league currently believes they won the night. Somewhere in Green Bay, a two-dollar running back is about to decide whether that belief was correct for at least one of you. We’ll find out about the rest in January — and about Jacobs, probably a lot sooner.
