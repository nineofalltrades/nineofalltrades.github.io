One Piece Of Math, I'll Be Quick

What VOR means, for those of you who skipped it

Here's the lie

Raw points make every quarterback look like a genius and every tenth-round running back look like a mistake. A 280-point quarterback can be replacement-level. A 180-point receiver can win you a week. If you grade a draft on points, you will congratulate yourselves for drafting the position that scores the most, which is how half of this league has been evaluating itself since Arian Foster.

What he scored− What the last startable guy at his position scored= VOR

The bar moves with the roster. 2011–2013 you started two receivers. 2014 you started three and added a flex. 2020 you got an IR slot. We measure every pick against this league's own replacement level, not some magazine's.

What it doesn't do. VOR grades the pick, not whether you started him. If you drafted a league-winner and sat him, the draft still gets credit and you still get roasted in a different section. Kickers and defenses are thrown out of the value math — one of each gets drafted per team, so "replacement" becomes whoever finished last and suddenly every kicker looks like a masterstroke.

Act I · 2011–2014

When the commissioner taught you how this works

Year one, Kulp takes CadillacKulpDeville to a title from draft slot 9 with the seventh-best draft in the room and negative draft value. Minus 53 VOR. The snake handed him the worst corner of the board and he still won, because this league has never been decided on draft night and the rest of you spent fourteen years refusing to believe it.

pat kulp won it in 2012. Michaeopteryx showed up in year two, named the team Human Psentipede, never changed it, and took 2013. Kulp went back-to-back-adjacent in 2014 — again with a below-average draft, again with 45 in-season moves, which is the part you should have written down.

The name never changed. CadillacKulpDeville. Fifteen seasons. One string of characters. Some of you have used that as a personality. He used it as a filing system.

Key Stat Two of the first four champions won with a draft that finished seventh or eighth in a ten-team league. The commissioner did it twice.
Act II · 2015–2016

Haefner repeats, and Kulp drafts a title he doesn't win

2015 is the year the tape gets cruel. Kulp posts 445.9 draft VOR — the best board in the league that season, still one of the four best drafts anyone has ever turned in here. Devonta Freeman in the tenth round, finished RB1, +176 surplus. Then he takes Eddie Lacy first overall-adjacent in round 1 and watches him finish RB32 at −97.

He finishes ninth.

Haefner wins the thing with −59 draft VOR, the sixth-best board in a ten-team league, and then he does it again in 2016 from slot 9 with the actual best draft of that year. Back-to-back. Medina Sod. The only repeat in league history.

Kulp had the receipts in August and lost them by December. That is not a draft problem. That is the whole sport, in one season, with the commissioner's name on it.

Key Stat Best draft of 2015: 445.9 VOR, finished 9th. Championship draft of 2015: −59 VOR. The gap between those two sentences is why I still have a job.
Act III · The Exhibit They Will Hate

The best drafter in league history never made a podium

senftiva lasted nine seasons. He averaged +173 draft VOR per year, the highest mark on the board. In 2018 he posted 671 VOR — the second-best single draft ever recorded in this league — and finished sixth. In 2012 he was +237 against the room and finished eighth. In 2013, +208, ninth.

Zero titles. Zero podiums. Average finish: 7.56.

And before anyone starts in about the schedule: he won 44.4% of his games against an all-play mark of 41.0%. The schedule did him a favor, worth +3.4 points of win rate, and he still never sniffed the trophy. Everything points at the lineup card.

Worst lineup efficiency in the league at 84.8%. He left 21.1 points on the bench every week — roughly 338 a season, which is more than a full extra starter. He drafted the rosters. He just didn't start them.

He had the best drafts in the room and the worst lineup in the league. That is not a paradox. That is a choice, repeated every Sunday. Nine seasons · 0 podiums · 84.8% efficiency
Act IV · 2017–2022

Then the 1.01 started killing people

From 2017 through 2022 the first overall pick failed six years in a row. Four of those six returned less than nothing — David Johnson, Le'Veon Bell, Christian McCaffrey twice, Jonathan Taylor. The player you burned the first name in the draft on never even finished inside the startable pool.

Al did it twice. Bell in 2015, Bell again in 2018. Then he took McCaffrey 1.01 in 2020 and watched him finish RB54, and somehow still came in second, which tells you everything about how little the first pick is worth and how much of Al's career has been surviving his own first round.

2019 is Kevin's year, and it is the funniest championship in the file. Best draft in the league: 445.6 VOR. Worst lineup efficiency of any champion: 85.08%, with 405.7 points left on the bench. He went 6–7 in the regular season against an all-play mark of 8.3 wins. He still won the trophy. He also owns the worst start/sit decision in league history, which we will get to, and which he will never hear the end of.

Natalie won 2020 the other way: 94.66% lineup efficiency, 132.9 points left on the bench all year, seven waiver moves. GooNads. Same name, every season, since 2011. Then she finished last, last, and last-adjacent for two years after, because this league does not let anyone have a clean narrative.

2021 is the one I would put in a museum. Mike posts 803 draft VOR — the best draft anyone has ever had here, Cooper Kupp in the fifth round at +254, the single most valuable pick in league history — and finishes second. Ryan, in his second season, takes McCaffrey 1.01, gets −112, and wins the championship anyway. Deebo Samuel in the eighth, +179. The schedule gifted him a 10–4 record against 7.2 wins deserved. Mike had the best board ever recorded. Ryan had the trophy. There is no version of this story where that is fair, and it happened anyway.

Key Stat Nine of fifteen champions had a bottom-half draft. Two had the best draft in the league. The weakest board ever to win it: Michaeopteryx, 2018, ninth of ten.
Act V · 2023–2025

Thirteen years of almost, then the chair in the corner

Kulp takes Justin Jefferson 1.01 in 2023. Jefferson finishes WR32, −83 surplus. Kulp wins the championship anyway, from the cursed chair, with the sixth-best draft, 40 waiver moves, 90% lineup efficiency. Third ring. Same team name. The man has not learned a new trick because he has not needed one.

2024 is when 9ofalltrades finally cashed it. Eight podium finishes. One title. Thirteen team names. The luckiest regular in the league at +3.2 win rate over all-play, which is a polite way of saying the schedule has been on his side for a decade and it still took him until year fourteen. Aichane 2.0. Sixth-best draft. Slot 2. You do not get to call it destiny when you had eight chances to end this sooner.

2025: Mike, slot 9, seventh-best draft, second ring, eight years after the first. Same chair Kulp won from in 2011 and Haefner won from in 2016 and pat kulp won from in 2022. Four championships from slot 9. Zero from slot 4. The formal test says that's noise — p = 0.204 — and I believe the test. I also believe four trophies from the same seat is a bit, and I am not going to be the one who kills it.

Key Stat 9ofalltrades: 8 podiums, 1 title, 13 names, 15 seasons. The most consistent almost in the history of this league.

The Bench

The start/sit crimes this league will not be allowed to forget

Lineup efficiency is the second-strongest thing we can measure against final standing, rho +0.353, right behind draft value. Across 150 team-seasons you collectively left 39,917 points on the bench. That is not a rounding error. That is a lifestyle.

Key Stat Kevin owns three of the twelve worst bench decisions ever recorded, including the record: Tyler Lockett 53.0, Chase Claypool 0.8, week 7 of 2020. A 52.2-point gap. He won a championship the year before. The two facts are related only in that they happened to the same person.

How many running backs you take in the first five is indistinguishable from a coin flip. Reaching versus waiting is a coin flip. Your draft slot is a coin flip, which is the good news, because it means the snake is fair. Roster construction is not where this league is won. Closing Argument

What fifteen years actually proved

Draft night is a head start, not a verdict. Two champions had the best draft in the league. Nine had a draft in the bottom half. Michaeopteryx won it from ninth on the board. Kulp won it from seventh with negative value. If your season died in November, August is not the reason and you know it.

The lineup card is the murder weapon. senftiva drafted like a professional and started like a man checking his phone at halftime. Kevin won a title with 405 points on the bench. Across this league you have left 39,917 points sitting. Kulp's 90.0% efficiency is the career leader and also the career leader in titles. That is not a coincidence I invented. That is the correlation.

Moves are the third rail and most of you won't touch it. Natalie averages 7.1 a year and has one ring to show for the discipline. Kulp averages 32.5 and has three. 9ofalltrades averages 34.5 and needed fourteen seasons to cash one. Activity is not a personality. It is a lever, and the people who pull it finish higher.

The 1.01 is a trap with a chair. Eleven of fifteen first overalls lost value. Six straight years it failed. Four times it returned nothing. Ryan won a title with a dead McCaffrey. Kulp won a title with a dead Jefferson. Stop treating the first name on the card like a birthright.

See you in September. Set your lineup.
