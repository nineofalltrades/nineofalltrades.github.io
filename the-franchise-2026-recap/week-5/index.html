The Franchise · Week 5 · By Mohn Jadden

Payback,
Plus Interest.

Thirteen seasons. Nearly a thousand games. Nobody in this league had ever hit 200 in the regular season, and this week D-Unit hung 207.20*, the HIGHEST SCORE IN LEAGUE HISTORY. The guy on the other side scored 197.06 and lost, which is also a league record, just the kind nobody frames. Meanwhile, across town, the first-place team won a game where neither side cracked 95. One game wrote history. The other game wrote an apology letter. Week 5 was a fireworks show with a dumpster fire going in the parking lot.

Dao set the all-time record and needed almost every decimal of it. Rob played a perfect lineup with a quarterback he found after midnight and lost anyway. Janice won with a score that would have lost to eight teams. Chad's backup quarterback had the best afternoon in the league from a folding chair. TJ beat the commissioner for the fifth time running. Eric's best receiver didn't make it through Thursday night. Curtis lost by a field goal's worth of loose change. Mike S rented a receiver for four days. Chris finally started the right quarterback, and that was the highlight. Mike E won by 37 and somehow only got half credit. Let's get into it.

The Story

Payback, Plus Interest

Start with Rob, because Rob's roster walked into Sunday on crutches. Lamar Jackson was ruled out with the ankle. Caleb Williams missed a third straight game with the hamstring. Saquon Barkley and Josh Jacobs never suited up, and Achane and Alec Pierce are parked on IR. So at 12:58 a.m. Saturday by Yahoo's clock, a $0 claim cleared for Kyler Murray, the same Kyler Murray that Curtis had cut at 4:29 a.m. Wednesday to make room for Keon Coleman. Rob's team was projected for 119.24. Nine of his ten starters beat their projection. Bucky Irving ran for a career-high 165 on Thursday night, Christian Watson torched the Bears for two long touchdowns, Kyler put up 21.66 in his first Rob start, and the waiting room finished with 197.06. That beat every other score in the league by at least 38.

And it lost by 10.14, because Dao picked this week to break the league. Jahmyr Gibbs ran for 151 and two scores in Arizona. Jaylen Warren turned a catch into an 80-yard touchdown, later broke a 50-yard touchdown run, and finished with 188 yards from scrimmage. Parker Washington caught two touchdowns in London. D-Unit: 207.20, the highest score this league has ever seen. Here's the part you can't make up. The old record was Rob's: 201.30 in the 2020 third-place game, and the team he dropped it on was Dao's. And the old record for highest losing score, 164.62, belonged to Dao, from that same 2020 postseason. Six years later Dao took Rob's record and handed him his old one. The all-time series is now 7–6 Rob, but Dao's not counting games anymore. He's counting receipts.

Awards

🏆 Weekly Hardware

📜 The Record Book Needs a New Page

D-Unit and Ken You Not Pickett combined for 404.26. The old league record for a single game was 343.90, set by TJ and Curtis in Week 3 of 2015. They beat it by 60.36, and before anybody brings up the second flex: the two-flex-era record was 339.10, Chad and Rob last year. Still lapped.

🎠 The Carousel Award

Justin Herbert started Weeks 1 and 2. Bryce Young started Weeks 3 and 4. Jacoby Brissett, a $0 claim, started Week 5 and threw three touchdowns at the Lions for 28.88, the best quarterback score Eric has gotten all year. Eric has changed quarterbacks like a guy changing lanes in traffic, and somehow the third lane is moving.

🏥 The Extended Warranty

Both Mikes went shopping and both bought damaged goods. Mike S grabbed Tre' Harris at 7:13 a.m. Friday, started him, lost him to a hamstring, and dropped him at 2:35 a.m. Tuesday. Mike E spent $17, the biggest bid of the week, on Will Shipley, who gave him 8.30 and left with a foot injury. Gallows humor only. Both Mikes won anyway. The return desk is closed.

📦 The Trade Deadline Came Early

Marvin Harrison Jr. was inactive for Arizona on Sunday, sitting on Chris's bench, when the Cardinals traded him to Houston during the first quarter. Chris's bench receiver changed teams mid-game without playing a snap for either one. Most guys need a full season to disappear like that.

🧹 The Clean Sheet

Janice's lineup scored 94.34. The best lineup she could have possibly set scored 95.34. She took a roster missing Mahomes and Kenneth Walker, used 99 percent of it, and walked out with first place. That's not luck. That's a woman who knows exactly how little effort it takes.

Start/Sit Court: no convictions, four weeks running. Every bench boom this week was projected below, or within three points of, the guy who started. But the jury has questions.

Curtis, please rise. You lost by 2.08. Alvin Kamara gave you 1.90 at flex. Sitting on your bench: Emeka Egbuka (14.30), Keon Coleman (7.20) and Keaton Mitchell (5.00). Any one of the three wins the game. Keaton. Mitchell. Five points. That's all it took. Not guilty, but your photo stays on the wall.

Chad, you too. Dalton Kincaid started at tight end and scored 3.20. Kyle Pitts (12.70) and Brenton Strange (10.30) sat behind him, and either one flips a 4.32-point loss. Kincaid projected higher than both, so it's a bad beat. You're free to go. The tight ends are not.

Everyone else is dismissed. Dao and Rob had perfect lineups, and Eric and Chris couldn't have covered their margins if they'd started every player they own. Court adjourned.

Mohn's Closing Thought

Five weeks in, this league has taught us one thing: there's no such thing as enough. Score 197 and you lose. Score 94 and you're in first. You don't need the best team in The Franchise. You need the right Sunday against the right guy, and nobody gets to pick which Sunday that is.

Special Shoutouts:

  • Dao, for keeping Jalen Hurts around as the most decorated backup quarterback in America.
  • Rob, for scoring 197 points without spending a single FAAB dollar all season. Frugal and furious.
  • Eric, for running the league and the waiver wire at the same time: 17 moves in five weeks, and the commish is still shopping.
  • TJ, for 16 moves of his own, including a Tyreek Hill pickup who's still listed as NA. Hope is a strategy, apparently.
  • Mike E, for 76.88 points from his quarterback and two receivers. The team name finally has a business plan.
  • Chris, for MarShawn Lloyd's 17.20 on the bench, projected under the guy who started. The bad-beat collection grows.

Set those Week 6 lineups, read the injury report twice, and may your starters stay healthy, your bench stay quiet, and your opponent have a merely good Sunday.
