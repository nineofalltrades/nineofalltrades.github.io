The Story

Bench the QB, Win the Game

Sherlock opened as the biggest favorite of the week. The board should ask for a refund.

The Annexation of Upset

Sherlock walked into Sunday priced like a coronation — a 27-point favorite, the fattest spread either projection system posted all week — and got run out of the building anyway. Jaxon Smith-Njigba did his part, 122 yards and a score worth 23.20 fantasy points, the single best individual line in the league. None of it mattered, because on the other sideline Sean McVay decided at halftime that Matthew Stafford (6.80 points, on a 20.45 projection) had seen enough football for one Sunday and benched him. Curtis's own franchise quarterback, benched by a man who doesn't even play in this league.

Curtis won anyway. D'Andre Swift, projected for a modest 12.93, put together a 34.70-point flex game that had no business happening. Tyler Loop — a kicker Curtis found on waivers for pocket change — dropped 14.00 points, more than some of Sherlock's actual skill-position players managed. Final: 136.60–125.60. Yahoo and Black Hat both had this one backwards by more than twenty points, and somewhere Mike E is explaining to people that his team got the best receiver performance in the league and still lost by eleven. Good luck with that one.

Awards

This Week's Hardware

Nobody handed the Awards desk a real mistake to point at this week, so the hardware goes to the football itself — and to one manager who deserves it just for the audacity.

The McVay Special

Curtis's own starting QB got benched at halftime for a 6.80-point embarrassment, by a coaching staff that has never heard of this league. Curtis won anyway, on a streamed kicker and a flex game nobody projected. Some managers get lucky. Curtis gets away with murder.

Worst Unit in Football

The Texans defense, started in good faith by HoldMyCHUBBBA, posted −2.00 fantasy points on a 6.14 projection — the week's only negative line, and a strong reminder that "defense" is doing a lot of work in that name.

The Understudy Bowl

Six backup quarterbacks — Caleb Williams (40.36), Tyler Shough (36.10), Trevor Lawrence (28.10), Jalen Hurts (26.82), Patrick Mahomes (24.86), Jayden Daniels (19.96) — combined for 176.20 points that spent the week exactly where nobody could use them. Six managers are rethinking their entire quarterback philosophy at 1am.

No rematch, just symmetry, and Mohn will take the coincidence anyway: last year's championship game was Nico'd & Dimed over the Annexation of PR. The two didn't play each other this week, but TJ's defending champs opened 0-2-0 the same week the team they beat for the title delivered the league's biggest upset on its way to 2-0-0. Funny how that works out.
Mohn's Closing Thought

Four of five point spreads got taken out back and shot in Week 1, and the board's own oddsmakers went 1-for-5 — which isn't a typo, that's just what happens when actual football players decline to cooperate with a spreadsheet. Nico'd & Dimed sat 5.52 points from an entirely different season, on a call that was right on paper and wrong in the box score — the most fantasy-football sentence ever written. Meanwhile the defending champs are 0-2-0 the same week the team they dethroned last November is 2-0-0 off the ugliest upset of the year — no rematch, just the universe having a laugh at TJ's expense. Somebody in this league is due for an easy week. It will not be next week, and it will not be announced in advance.
