The Method

No more projections. Just points.

August’s grades had to guess. This page doesn’t. Every drafted player is scored on his actual 2026 Yahoo fantasy points through Week 10 (Half-PPR, this league’s settings), measured against a replacement-level player at his position — the same value-over-replacement math the Chronicle uses.

Ten teams start eight skill players: QB, two RB, two WR, TE and two W/R/T flex spots. Fill every starting slot with the best scorers in the league and the next man up sets the bar: the 11th QB (210.3 pts), the 31st RB (72.9), the 31st WR (76.5) and the 11th TE (72.1). Everything above that line is value. Everything below it is a waiver claim with a price tag.

To turn points into dollars, the room’s own auction money gets spread across all the value that actually got produced. This year, every point over replacement was worth $0.48. A player’s “earned” price is $1 plus his points over replacement times that rate. Profit is earned minus paid.

House rulesPoints count no matter who rostered the player, so a guy who got cut still earns for the team that drafted him — this grades the auction, not the waiver wire. Kickers and defenses are excluded, same as August. Missed games count against you: a $44 running back who plays three games is a $44 running back who played three games.

The Receipts · How August aged

The model can smell an overpay. It can’t find a bargain.

Each team got one “Overpay” call and one “Best Value” call in August. Ten weeks later:

Overpay calls
9 of 10 right
Hampton, Amon-Ra, Price, Egbuka, McConkey, Jordan Mason, TreVeyon Henderson, Lawrence and MarShawn Lloyd all lost money. The one miss: Bhayshul Tuten, called a $20 overpay, now +$19.9.
Best Value calls
3 of 10 right
Josh Allen (+$8.2), Trey McBride (+$7.4) and Cam Skattebo (+$4.5) paid off. Drake London, Rashee Rice and Breece Hall lost money; the rest were roughly break-even.
Grade vs. haul
A → 7th, F → 5th
The top-graded draft has the 7th-best return. The bottom-graded draft has the 5th-best return and a top-3 record.

That’s the lesson of the 2026 auction. Fear was priced correctly. Hope wasn’t priced at all. Nobody in August saw a $6 Chuba Hubbard, a $4 Joe Burrow or a $3 Matthew Golden coming, and those three picks are worth $115 of profit combined.

Closing Argument
The auction decided who could score. The schedule decided who won. D-Unit won the first one by a hundred dollars and is still fourth.

Four regular-season weeks left. The draft built D-Unit, and the model says D-Unit wins it. Everyone else is playing for the 42%, and that comes down to your bench, your waiver claims and your Sunday lineup decisions.
