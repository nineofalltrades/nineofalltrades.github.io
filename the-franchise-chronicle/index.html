The Recap You Asked For

The VOR tape

Positions are not on the same scale, and this room still drafts like they are. In 2021 it took 365.6 points to be the tenth-best quarterback and 155.6 to be the twenty-fifth-best receiver. A 300-point quarterback was below replacement. A 300-point receiver was +144. That is why $4 Josh Allen and $130 Miles Sanders are not the same kind of stupid.

Josh Allen, $4 in 2020: +167. Miles Sanders, $130 the same night: +34.5. One of them cost thirty-two times more. Kickers and defenses still count against the budget. They earn you nothing. The bar moved when we went to twelve and again when you added the second flex. Every draft number below uses it. If you want the formula, you already know it.

Act I · 2014–2017

When we were all poor and sensible

Ten managers, no keepers, nobody knew anything. The most expensive man on the board that first night was LeSean McCoy at $68, and for three more years nobody topped $72. Then keepers arrived in 2015 and average top-three spending concentration fell from 66% to 44% in a single year. With the stars locked up, draft night became a scrap fight. Some of you still have not recovered from that feeling.

Act II · 2018

Dao breaks the ceiling

Draft night, 2018. The record bid in this league is $72.

Dao says one hundred and five dollars. For a rookie. A rookie who has never played a down of professional football.

The room lets him have it.

Saquon Barkley scores 373.4 points. +226 VOR. It is still, seven years later, the best big-money pick anybody has ever made here.

And it wasn't even a one-year win. Dao kept Barkley five more seasons — six years, $247 total, +324 lifetime. He didn't buy a player. He bought a lease.

Key Stat$105 was 46% above the previous record bid in league history. Nobody has topped +226 since, and eight people have tried.
Act III · 2019–2023

Then everybody lost their minds

Ten times in twelve years somebody has paid $80 or more for one player. Eight of them came after Saquon, and every single one was a running back until 2024.

2020 was the peak of the sickness: three separate $130 bids in one night. TJ on Clyde Edwards-Helaire. Mike S on Jonathan Taylor. Chad on Miles Sanders. Two rookies and a guy in his second year, $390 of a $2,000 room, gone before most of you had a running back.

Then in 2021 Curtis looked at all that and decided it wasn't enough. $145 on Najee Harris. Seventy-two percent of his budget on one man, and then eleven of his last fourteen roster spots filled with dollar players. It worked. That is the infuriating part.

Act IV · 2024–2025

The fever breaks

Nobody called a meeting. Nobody admitted anything. The bidding just quietly stopped.

In 2024 the biggest price of the night went to a wide receiver for only the second time in twelve years — $91 for Marvin Harrison Jr., the first since Odell Beckham topped the 2015 board at $42. In 2025 the top bid fell to $62, the lowest since 2015, and dollar players jumped back up to 50.

And then, in the same season the league finally started behaving itself, TJ won the whole thing from 9th of 10 on draft night. We have been calling it the eighth-best draft. We were being generous. The weakest draft ever to end in a championship.

Exhibit E · Eleven Deals

This league does not trade. Eleven unique deals in twelve years, five of them in 2014 when you still thought this was a friendly. Nobody has traded since 2023, which is either a policy or a personality. I have a guess.
