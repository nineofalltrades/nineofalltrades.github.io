One Piece Of Math, I'll Be Quick

What VOR means, for those of you who skipped it

Every number below is VOR — value over replacement. You need it because raw points lie to you.

Here's the lie

In 2021 you needed 366 points just to be the tenth-best quarterback. A guy anybody could have had off waivers. You needed 112 to be the thirty-fifth-best receiver. So a 300-point quarterback was below average and a 300-point receiver won you the league. If you judge a draft on raw points you will conclude every quarterback is a masterstroke, which is how half of you have been evaluating yourselves for a decade.

Here's the fix

What he scored−What the last startable guy at his position scored=VOR

The bar moves with the position and with the league. Ten teams start ten quarterbacks and about twenty-five running backs, so the bar sits at QB10 and RB25. We had twelve teams in 2016 and 2017, so it moved. We added a second flex in 2025, so it moved again.

2021, so you can't argue

Ja'Marr Chase: 275.8 points. Replacement receiver: 112.3. +163.5. Cost $1.
Najee Harris: 298.4 points. Replacement back: 158.6. +139.8. Cost $145.

Najee scored 22.6 more points and cost 145 times more money and was worth less. That is the whole reason this number exists, and Curtis has never recovered.

What it doesn't do

VOR gives you full credit for a guy's whole season even if you cut him in week three. It grades the pick, not your lineup calls. Your start/sit crimes are a separate trial. Kickers and defenses are thrown out entirely — one gets drafted per team, so "replacement" becomes the worst kicker anyone took and suddenly every kicker looks like a genius buy. The money you burn on them still counts against you. It just earns you nothing.

Act I · 2014–2017

When we were all poor and sensible

Ten managers, no keepers, nobody knew anything. The most expensive man on the board that first night was LeSean McCoy at $68, and for three more years nobody topped $72. It was a simpler time. We were better people.

Then keepers arrived in 2015 and the auction went flat overnight — average top-three spending concentration fell from 66% to 44% in a single year, the biggest one-season change this league has ever recorded. With the stars locked up before anyone sat down, draft night stopped being about stars and started being about scraps.

It was also the last time this league made any sense. From 2016 through 2019 the manager with the best draft won the championship. Four years running. Cause, effect, trophy. We have not managed it since.

Key StatFive champions in twelve years had the best draft in the league. Four of them came in a row, and then it stopped happening.
Act II · 2018

Dao breaks the ceiling

Draft night, 2018. The record bid in this league is $72.

Dao says one hundred and five dollars. For a rookie. A rookie who has never played a down of professional football.

The room lets him have it.

Saquon Barkley scores 373.4 points. +226 VOR. It is still, seven years later, the best big-money pick anybody has ever made here.

And here is the thing nobody wants to admit: it wasn't even a one-year win. Dao kept Barkley five more seasons — six years, $247 total, +324 lifetime. He didn't buy a player. He bought a lease.

Key Stat$105 was 46% above the previous record bid in league history. Nobody has topped +226 since, and eight people have tried.
Act III · 2019–2023

Then everybody lost their minds

Ten times in twelve years somebody has paid $80 or more for one player. Eight of them came after Saquon, and every single one was a running back until 2024.

2020 was the peak of the sickness: three separate $130 bids in one night. TJ on Clyde Edwards-Helaire. Mike S on Jonathan Taylor. Chad on Miles Sanders. Two rookies and a guy in his second year, $390 of a $2,000 room, gone before most of you had a running back.

Then in 2021 Curtis looked at all that and decided it wasn't enough. $145 on Najee Harris. Seventy-two percent of his budget on one man, and then eleven of his last fourteen roster spots filled with dollar players. It worked. That is the infuriating part. It worked.

Judge those by year one and they look like coin flips. Judge them by what they became and it's a different story — in a keeper league a giant bid isn't a one-year bet, it's the price of a multi-year lease. Dao's $105 Saquon became six seasons. His $103 Gibbs has already returned +481 across three.

Which makes the misses worse, not better. TJ's $130 Edwards-Helaire got kept twice more and finished three seasons and $150 in the hole at −76. Curtis paid $95 for Javonte Williams, watched him tear a knee in week four, and kept him anyway. A bad expensive pick doesn't expire in this format. You have to choose to end it, and none of you ever do.

Key StatJavonte Williams, 2022: $95, 40.7 points, −134 VOR. The worst pick in the recorded history of this league, and it belongs to a man with zero championships.
Act IV · 2024–2025

The fever breaks

Nobody called a meeting. Nobody admitted anything. The bidding just quietly stopped.

In 2024 the biggest price of the night went to a wide receiver for only the second time in twelve years — $91 for Marvin Harrison Jr., the first since Odell Beckham topped the 2015 board at $42. In 2025 the top bid fell to $62, the lowest since 2015, and dollar players jumped back up to 50.

2025 also gave us a second flex spot, sixteen roster slots, and one more starter at running back and receiver on every team — which is why the value numbers that season are measured against a deeper bar than everything before it.

And then, in the same season the league finally started behaving itself, TJ won the whole thing with the eighth-best draft in a ten-team league. The weakest draft ever to end in a championship. Twelve years of careful auction strategy, and the trophy went to a man who finished eighth on draft night.

The Bonfire

Every one of you burns a quarter of it

Money spent on players who finished below replacement level — dollars that bought you something a free agent would have given you for nothing. Across twelve seasons this league has set fire to $5,807.

And before anyone gets smug: the best of you wastes 19.8% and the worst wastes 26.5%. Six and a half points separate the entire league. You are all equally bad at this. It is the only thing that unites you.

Closing Argument

What twelve years actually proved

Draft night is a head start, not a verdict. Five champions had the best draft in the league. Six had a draft in the bottom half. Two of you have won it all from seventh place on draft night and one of you did it from eighth. If your season died in November, the auction is not the reason and you know it.

The shape of your roster is worth nothing. Stars-and-scrubs, balanced, all running backs, all receivers — across 124 team-seasons it has no relationship whatsoever to where you finish. Twelve years of draft-night strategy arguments in the group chat, and the numbers say every one of them was noise. Who you got mattered. How you arranged the money never did.

The long holds are everything. Travis Kelce for nine years. Derrick Henry for eight. McCaffrey for eight. Mahomes for six at a dollar a year plus change. Every trophy in this league's history has a lease behind it, and the managers who keep winning are the ones who identified a man early and then refused to be talked out of him.

See you in August. Bring money.
