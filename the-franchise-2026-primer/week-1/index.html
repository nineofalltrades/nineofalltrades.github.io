Wednesday & Thursday

The Rams picked a bad week to be bad.

Two games down, and one of them did more damage to this league than the other nine will combined.

Seahawks 13, Patriots 10 · Wednesday

Seattle needed a clean two-score night from its specialists to hold on, and got exactly that — Jason Myers made both his field-goal tries and the extra point, a tidy 7.00-point night that's currently the entire scoreboard for Nico'd & Dimed. The other story from that building was Jaxon Smith-Njigba, who turned 8 catches into 122 yards and a touchdown, the single best fantasy performance turned in by anyone this week — and, as it happens, the entire live score currently sitting on Sherlock's side of the ledger (see below).

New England's night was worse than the final score suggests. Drake Maye was fine — 178 pass yards, 47 more on the ground, one touchdown — solid, unspectacular, the kind of floor that TEAM HoldMyCHUBBBA drafted him for. A.J. Brown's night ended early in the third quarter: his ankle got trapped awkwardly between the turf and a Seattle corner going for a low throw, and he didn't come back. Three catches, 26 yards, and a 34-yard pass interference draw is what Chad has to show for his WR1 for what's looking like the next month. More on that below.

49ers 27, Rams 7 · Thursday

San Francisco didn't just win this one, it made Los Angeles look unprepared for the season to have started. Christian McCaffrey's workload never translated to production — 12.30 points, well off his own number, in a game his team won by 20. On the other side, the collapse was total. Matthew Stafford finished 155 yards and an interception before Sean McVay pulled him with the outcome decided; for what it's worth, that wasn't an injury, McVay just didn't need him anymore. Puka Nacua managed 74 yards on 5 catches and nothing to show in the end zone. The only Ram to beat his number was Kyren Williams, whose one late touchdown (41 rush yards, 24 more through the air) salvaged a mildly-positive day out of an otherwise dead building. The kicker and defense didn't even get that much — Harrison Mevis was limited to a single extra point, and the Rams defense allowed 27 while picking off one pass.

The fantasy math on that single football game is brutal: it directly touched five of this league's ten rosters — Stafford and Nacua for The Annexation of PR, McCaffrey for The McClusterfucks, both the kicker and the defense for Henrys Heros, and Davante Adams's quiet 3-catch, 26-yard day for Ken You Not Pickett. Kyren Williams, propping up J Squad, is the only manager who left that building with anything to be happy about.

Waiver Wire

Dao made the boldest move of the week, and it cost him a dollar.

Two days before kickoff, and one manager already blinked hard.

The stunner
Kelce, $1
D-Unit cut Travis Kelce loose entirely and spent a dollar on Michael Mayer instead — from the same manager who put 54% of his auction cap into Jahmyr Gibbs and Ja'Marr Chase this summer. Bold, or a man who knows something about a 37-year-old's snap count the rest of us don't.

The Annexation of PR dropped Ricky Pearsall (SF WR, on IR) for Tre Tucker, swapped the Bills defense for the Lions, and traded Wil Lutz for Tyler Loop at kicker — a full lineup reshuffle capping off a rough 48 hours that also included Stafford and Nacua both busting Thursday.

Injury Watch

One real problem, two to keep an eye on.

The real oneA.J. Brown (NE WR, HoldMyCHUBBBA) suffered a high ankle sprain in the third quarter against Seattle when his foot got trapped under a defender going for a low throw. X-rays came back negative for a fracture, but the expectation is at least four weeks out. Chad's WR1 is gone for essentially a month right as the season starts.

Malik Nabers (NYG WR, Sherlock — a $21 keeper) spent the week playing coy about his own availability for Sunday's game against Dallas. The latest reporting has him trending toward playing, but it's worth checking his status again before Mike E's flex spot is locked in.

Jeremiyah Love (ARI RB, The Annexation of PR) is working back from a preseason ankle injury and is officially a game-time decision for Sunday's early game against the Chargers — worth a lineup check for Curtis before kickoff, on top of everything else that's gone wrong for him this week.

For the record: Matthew Stafford is not hurt. Sean McVay pulled him because the game was decided, not because of an injury. Small comfort for Curtis — the fantasy line doesn't care why.

Final Word

Two games down, eight to go.

The Rams did more to this league in one Thursday night than most bye weeks manage all season — five rosters touched, one quarterback benched by his own coach, and a keeper WR held to single digits. Nobody's record means a thing yet, but the board's already telling two of you the wrong story about who's winning.

Sunday sorts out the rest of it. Set your lineups, check Nabers and Love one more time before kickoff, and enjoy the fact that somewhere in this league, a Hall of Fame tight end is sitting on waivers for a dollar and nobody's claimed him yet.
