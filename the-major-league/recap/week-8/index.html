The Story

Two Quarterbacks, Zero Points, and an Empty Chair at the Head of the Table

The first-place team found out what happens when both halves of a backup plan fail on the same afternoon. The commissioner was happy to take the seat.

Kevin Had a Plan B. Plan B Got Benched.

Last week this column ended its injury report with three words: Fix that, Kevin. Lamar was out, Stroud was on bye, and BlueGreen had no healthy quarterback. Kevin fixed it. His 3:55 a.m. Wednesday waiver claim landed Jacoby Brissett, projected 19.30, and then Baltimore cleared Lamar to come back in Buffalo, so Kevin did the obvious thing and started the former MVP. Lamar's first pass fell incomplete. On the follow-through his right thumb hit a defender's hand, and that was his day: one snap, 0.00 points, thumb surgery and injured reserve. Tyler Huntley came in and got hurt too, the Ravens finished with emergency third-stringer Joe Fagnano, and the Bills blitzed on 64.5% of their snaps. Zay Flowers, projected 18.58, saw five targets from that carousel and caught none of them.

The insurance policy didn't pay out either. Brissett went 10 of 23 for 119 yards and an interception in Dallas before Arizona pulled him for Gardner Minshew in the third quarter, which is how Kevin's bench quarterback scored 4.46 and still beat his starter. Hunter Henry left with a foot injury in the second quarter (6.00), Drake London caught one ball for eight yards in a rain-soaked 16–0 shutout (1.80), and Spencer Shrader kicked one extra point (1.00). Ja'Marr Chase (31.10) and Jaylen Warren (21.30) scored more than half the team's points. The final was 118.98–100.40 to Medina Sod, the lowest score of the week, and BlueGreen fell from first to fifth. None of it was a lineup mistake. Lamar was the right start, he got hurt on the first play, and the backup would have been worth 4.46 in a game decided by 18.58. Kevin did exactly what this column asked, and the column apologizes. C.J. Stroud is back from his bye this week, which makes him the healthiest quarterback Kevin owns by default.

The Commissioner Takes the Chair. The Champ Holds the Door.

Brian Kulp, meanwhile, had the kind of Sunday that makes a commissioner want to send a league-wide email. Joe Burrow threw for 462 yards and five touchdowns, gave three interceptions back to one Titans safety, then won it in overtime himself on a 9-yard scramble: 42.48 points, the best of the week by anyone. Per NFL Research, he's only the second quarterback ever with 450-plus passing yards, five touchdown passes and a rushing touchdown in a game, after Norm Van Brocklin in 1951. Jahmyr Gibbs ran for 153 and a score (24.80), Matthew Golden added 20.90, and CadillacKulpDeville finished at 169.08, the week's high by 17.38. Brian still left 17.60 on the bench (Jonah Coleman 20.40, Luther Burden 18.60), because he is who he is, and he's now at 192.90 for the season, still the league's bench champion. But five teams are 5-3 and Brian has more points than any of them, so the commissioner is in first place. The group chat is going to hear about this.

The guy holding the door was WhoseHungry?. Mike scored 111.58 and lost by 57.50, which pushed his points against to 1,286.04, the most through eight weeks in league history. The old mark was pat kulp's 2021 OJ no Kulp at 1,250.60, with Al's 2018 absALute dominationA 0.34 behind it, and Mike has now lapped both by 35-plus. He also had the best running back of the week and didn't know it: Tyler Allgeier ran for 147 yards and caught a touchdown for 36.10 on Mike's bench, a job he only got because Jeremiyah Love, also on Mike's bench, hurt his ankle again. Allgeier was projected 7.28 to Zach Charbonnet's 10.31, so nobody's blaming Mike for starting Charbonnet (5.90). DK Metcalf caught one pass for four yards and dropped another into a Browns defender's hands. Bucky Irving ran for 170 and Tyler Bass kicked 16.00, and it was still a 57-point loss. The champ is 2-6 and ninth, and next week he plays his wife.
