The Story

A Win, Barely, and a Bench, Again

The defending champion found a pulse, and the first-place team found out it's not the only one with a good bench.

WhoseHungry? Gets a Plate

Five weeks in, Mike finally got the night he's been owed. On Thursday in Dallas, Bucky Irving ran 21 times for 165 yards, caught a touchdown and scored a rushing one, good for 31.50. In the same game, Mike Evans put up 26.30 on the other side of the field from Mike's own CeeDee Lamb, who caught two balls for nine yards and left late with a quad bruise (2.90). Mike played both ends of a Bucs-Cowboys game and won the half that counted. Then came Sunday: Matthew Stafford, the 5:20 a.m. quarterback everyone laughed at last week, threw for 27.32. Tyler Warren added 21.40, Ka'imi Fairbairn kicked for 17, and the Texans defense chipped in 15. The defending champion is 1-4. It's the worst record in the league that has ever felt this good.

Justin Haefner did nearly everything right and lost anyway, for the second straight week. Jayden Daniels scored 32.26, Parker Washington 30.40, Malik Nabers 29.10 and JSN 22.80. Medina Sod scored 155.46, the third-best total of the week. But Isaiah Likely caught 11 balls for 94 yards and two touchdowns in the Giants' 27–27 tie with Washington, and he did it on the bench: 32.40 points, with Tucker Kraft (11.60) in the TE slot. By the pre-game numbers that was a coin flip, 11.59 to 11.22, and Haefner called it the way most people would. Then there's the backfield. With Kenneth Walker on bye and Breece Hall out, his starting running backs were Alvin Kamara and Mike Washington Jr.: 4.60 combined. The Steelers defense held Indianapolis without an offensive touchdown and still scored 1.00, because nobody turned the ball over. The Texans D scored 15. That's a 14-point swing in a game decided by 1.76. Medina Sod has scored 306.10 points in two straight losses. That's not a slump. That's a curse with good stats.

Kevin Sits the Running Back. Again.

Last week Kevin benched Kyren Williams and Kyle Monangai, who combined for 64.70, and the projections backed him up. This week the projections didn't. Jaylen Warren was projected 19.56, the highest of any running back on the roster. Aaron Jones (15.10) and Kyren (17.80) started. Warren went out against the Colts and caught an 80-yard touchdown, then ran for a 50-yard one, and became the first NFL player since Herschel Walker in 1986 to do both in one game. 36.80 on the bench. Two weeks, 101.50 points of running back on the pine.

And BlueGreen won by 28.68 with the high score of the week, 181.24, because Christian Watson scored 32.30 and new kicker Spencer Shrader hit for 21 a week after his predecessor scored 2. Lamar was out with the ankle, so Kevin grabbed C.J. Stroud on Friday afternoon, five days after Mike dumped him at 5:20 a.m., and Stroud gave him 20.94. The lineup Kevin could have played was worth 211.24. The lineup he did play is in first place at 4-1. Kevin is the only manager in this league who can leave 30 points on the bench and still post the week's top score. That's either the best roster in the league or a warning about what happens when he starts setting it correctly.

Mohn's Closing Thought
The week the defending champion finally won, he needed a first-place team to bench 32 points and play a 1-point defense to do it. Kevin is in first because he's so loaded he can bench his best running back two weeks running and still win. Meanwhile pat set a perfect lineup and lost, TJ made the smartest pickup of the week and lost, and Michaeopteryx did exactly what he was told and lost. Fantasy football is not a meritocracy. It's a raffle where Kevin bought most of the tickets.
