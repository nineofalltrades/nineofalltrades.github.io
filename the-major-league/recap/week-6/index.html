The Story

One Curse Lifted, Two Homework Assignments Graded

Haefner finally got paid for scoring a lot of points. The two managers who did exactly what this column told them to do got nothing.

Medina Sod Plays the Perfect Game

For two weeks, Justin Haefner was the best team in the league that kept losing. He'd scored 306.10 in back-to-back losses, he'd left 32.40 points of Isaiah Likely on his bench, and his defense had scored 1. This week he started Likely, and Likely scored 23.90. He started Kenneth Walker, back from the bye, and Walker ran in two touchdowns against the Chargers for 27.90. Malik Nabers caught 12 balls for 165 yards in a loss to the Saints and scored 28.50. JSN added 25.40, Jayden Daniels 21.00, and the Steelers defense that cost him last week's game went for 10. Chris Boswell, picked up at 4:23 a.m. Wednesday to replace a Will Reichard on bye, kicked for 18. The full total was 188.70, which was also exactly what his best possible lineup would have scored. Every slot right. Even the flex, where Josh Downs and the benched Tucker Kraft both scored 8.70, was a dead heat he couldn't have lost.

The poor guy on the other side of it was Ryan, who did nothing wrong at all. Van Tine also set a perfect lineup. Tre Tucker, claimed off waivers Wednesday, caught for 26.70 on a 9.88 projection, and the Broncos D chipped in 12. It got him to 116.90, because Josh Allen scored 18.30 on a 26.34 projection, Tony Pollard managed 7.60 and Terry McLaurin was carted off with a knee injury at 3.70. Two managers each played the best lineup they had, and the game was decided by 71.80. That's not a coaching battle. That's a talent audit. Haefner is 4-2, first place and the league's top scorer by 64.40. The curse isn't gone. It just ran into a week it couldn't do anything about.

The Advice Column Goes 0-for-2

Last week this recap told Brian Kulp to do his homework, because Burrow, Gibbs, Jefferson and the Vikings D were all in his lineup and all on bye. He did it. All four went to the bench. He grabbed the Rams D on Thursday night and Germie Bernard on Wednesday, and he went from a 51.68-point Tuesday underdog to a 17.51-point kickoff underdog. Then Rhamondre Stevenson scored 3.60, Denzel Boston scored 2.50, Brandon Aubrey kicked for 1, and the Rams D scored 3. Germie Bernard scored 25.70 from the bench, where he sat behind Boston on a projection a point lower. Kulp lost by 25.16. He showed his work. He just got the answer wrong.

Kevin got the same note: start the running backs. With Aaron Jones on bye and Kyle Monangai out, he started Kyren Williams and Jaylen Warren, his two highest-projected backs, for once. They scored 19.50 combined, on 36.53 projected. Juwan Johnson caught zero passes at tight end for 0.00. Pat Freiermuth, claimed Friday afternoon, gave 3.10 at flex. The Bills defense gave 1. C.J. Stroud scored 23.98 and was the only thing standing between BlueGreen and double digits from half the lineup. The last time Kevin left 30 points on his bench, he won by 28. This time he left 14.80 and lost by 27.44 to the last-place team. GooNads beat him with Christian McCaffrey (31.70) and Carnell Tate (19.00). Kevin finally set his lineup the right way and scored half of what he did when he set it wrong. He's never going to listen to anyone again, and honestly, he's earned that.
