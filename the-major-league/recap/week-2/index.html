The Story

Never Rename Your Team After a Tuesday

A team name that survived fifteen seasons didn't survive one good Sunday. The Bears noticed.

Three Points at Soldier Field

Last week the Bears were the whole engine of the league's scariest roster: Caleb Williams went for 37.26, Chicago dropped 59 on Carolina, and Kulp rolled up 192.26. So Kulp did the thing you never do, and made it official — the CadillacKulpDeville name that ran every season from 2011 through 2025 is now CadillacKulpDaBears, and he doubled down on waivers Friday night by claiming Rome Odunze. Then Minnesota came to Soldier Field and held Chicago to a single field goal in a 9–3 loss. Three red-zone trips, zero touchdowns. A fumble at the one-yard line. A blocked 23-yard field goal. A final play where the tight end was told to block. The Bears ran for 289 yards in Week 1 and 134 in Week 2, and Kulp had a front-row seat for every inch of the difference.

Here's the Chicago wing of Kulp's lineup: Caleb Williams 8.72 on an 18.76 projection, Luther Burden 6.20 on 11.57, Colston Loveland 1.30 on 11.43. That's 16.22 points from the three Bears starters against 41.76 projected, with the new guy, Odunze, sitting on the bench for 7.30. For the full effect, Justin Jefferson was on the winning side of that game and still gave Kulp just 8.50. Then, late in the fourth, Caleb pulled up on a non-contact scramble — the MRI says Grade 1 hamstring strain, no tear, week-to-week, with Tyson Bagent lined up for Monday night against Philadelphia. He's the only quarterback on Kulp's roster. The worst part is that the bench had a winning lineup on it: Denzel Boston (20.50), Jonah Coleman (14.80) and Xavier Worthy (13.50) would have pushed the best possible score to 131.52, past Cha$ing Dubs' 123.70. But every one of them was projected below the starter he'd have replaced. Kulp read the board right. He just read it in a Bears jersey.

The Streak Is Dead. Long Live Al.

Let the record show: Al's last win before Sunday came in Week 14 of 2024, when year_of_the_deAL beat Kevin's Blue Green 126.76–109.32. That was more than 21 months ago. Then came 2025 as ALBERTosaurus: 0-14. Fourteen regular-season games, fourteen losses, by an average of 53.39 points, on 100.83 points a week. The closest he got was 5.60 in Week 1. The worst was a 111.36-point beating from OJ no Kulp in Week 12. Then Week 1 of 2026 made it fifteen in a row. The Week 1 recap said nobody was saying it was happening again. It was starting to look like it was.

And then the universe cleared a path. Al woke up Sunday, grabbed Brock Purdy off the wire at 4:53 a.m. by Yahoo's clock, and started him over Joe Burrow for 28.48. George Kittle came back from last week's injury for 18.00. Harrison Butker kicked in 17. And the opponent laid down like a welcome mat. TJ's Bijan Claude Van Damme had Nico Collins on IR, Brock Bowers ruled out, and Michael Pittman Jr. ruled out. Five of his seven reserve spots scored zero. The guys who did play put up 79.96, the lowest score in the league, 21.86 below anybody else. The Week 1 recap called TJ the unluckiest manager in the league. This week the injuries were real, but they don't explain Bijan's 11.10, a one-target Marvin Harrison Jr., a 2-point kicker or the 1-point Chiefs defense TJ picked up Friday. Al didn't just end the streak. He ended it against the league's worst team, and he didn't have to sweat.
