The Story

Wrong Guys, Right Projections

One manager did everything the numbers told him to and lost by a field goal's worth of kicker. Another manager is making history, and not the good kind.

Kevin and the Furniture Showroom

Kevin set a perfectly defensible lineup. Jaylen Warren was projected 18.13, Aaron Jones 14.91, Drake London 16.81 at flex. Kyren Williams was projected 13.58 and sat. Then Kyren went to Philadelphia, caught ten passes, ran for two touchdowns and scored 36.70. Meanwhile Kyle Monangai, projected 9.13, carried the ball 30 times for 146 yards and two more touchdowns in the Bears' win over the Jets, good for 28.00, also on the bench, and hurt his thumb doing it. Kevin's starting running backs scored 32.40 combined. His benched ones scored 64.70. By the pre-game numbers, he got it right. By the final whistle, he'd left a top-two lineup in the drawer: 170.08 possible, 122.98 played.

It still should have been fine. Ja'Marr Chase was out after the first half with a concussion, so Kevin's WR1 gave him 5.70, and that's not his fault. Christian Watson's 7.70 is a bit more his problem. But the real kicker is the kicker: Chase McLaughlin scored 2 for BlueGreen while Van Tine's Cam Little scored 14, a 12-point swing in a game decided by 2.14. Kevin also swapped the Eagles defense for the Bills at 9:33 Sunday morning by Yahoo's clock and got 4 points out of the trade. Van Tine played 125.12 out of a possible 129.42, the most efficient lineup in the league this week, and takes the win by doing the boring thing well. Kevin goes to 3-1 with the most expensive bench in the league this week, 104.10. Nobody sat on a bench that nice in a Pottery Barn.

The Defending Champion Hits a New Low

Last week's preview called TJ vs. Mike "The Former Champions' Support Group." Only one of them needed the meeting. TJ scored 186.22, the best score in the league by 19.90, behind Nico Collins' two touchdowns (30.80), Bijan's 27.70, Tee Higgins' 26.70, Brock Bowers' 20.60, and Emanuel Wilson, a Seahawks running back projected at 9.02, who scored a rushing touchdown and a receiving touchdown out of the flex for 27.00. That's the kind of week where you can start Jakobi Meyers (6.30) and Cam Skattebo (7.60) and nobody notices.

Mike's week got worse in the morning. At 5:20 a.m. Sunday by Yahoo's clock, WhoseHungry? dropped C.J. Stroud and picked up Matthew Stafford. Stroud scored 23.08 for somebody's waiver wire. Stafford scored 11.68 for Mike. Saquon Barkley hurt his hamstring in the first half and finished with 2.00. CeeDee Lamb caught 17 passes for 189 yards and the winning touchdown in Houston, 41.30 points, and WhoseHungry? still lost by 64.94. That's 0-4. In 15 seasons of league records, no defending champion has ever started 0-4. Not one. And WhoseHungry? has allowed 676.74 points, the most any team has given up through four weeks in league history, edging out 2018's Human Psentipede (666.20). The 2025 trophy is still in the house. Where in the house, sources say, depends on the week and on who moved it last.

How Week 3's Hot Takes Aged

Week 3 said GooNads vs. Human Psentipede: "If not now, when?" Verdict: Now

GooNads won 120.50–101.96 for the first win of the year. It took a projection flip (Yahoo had Psentipede by Sunday), an inactive starter and Romeo Doubs scoring twice, but a W is a W. The household is on the board.

Mohn's Closing Thought

This was the week correct lost. Kevin read the projections right and left 64.70 on his bench. Medina Sod scored 150 and lost. Meanwhile GooNads started an empty jersey and won, Al started the tight end a stranger told him to and won by 48, and the defending champion made history by giving up more points through four weeks than any team this league has ever seen. Fantasy football doesn't reward you for being right. It rewards you for being lucky in public.
