The Story

Decimal Points and Second Thoughts

Three losing teams had a winning lineup sitting on the bench this week. Two of them are going to be thinking about it until Thursday.

The Best Seat in the House

Al's afternoon had everything a winning afternoon needs. Brock Purdy, the Sunday-morning pickup who ended the 15-game streak last week, threw for 297 yards and four touchdowns in a 36–30 win over Arizona, good for 31.28. Two of those touchdowns went to George Kittle, who finished with six catches for 82 yards and 26.20 points. Al owns Kittle. Al did not start Kittle. Kittle was questionable coming in after the Week 1 injury, Yahoo had him projected 0.19 ahead of Dalton Schultz, and Al went with the tight end who wasn't on the injury report. Schultz caught three balls for 30 yards and 6.00. That's a coin flip, not a crime. It's also 20.20 points in a game Al lost by 0.42.

It gets worse the longer you look at it. Al picked up the Patriots defense on Wednesday afternoon, and on Sunday New England lost 35–6 in Jacksonville and scored −1. An empty DEF slot would have won the game by 0.58. Bhayshul Tuten ran for a touchdown on the bench for 17.00. The best lineup Al could have fielded was 154.58, which beats every team in the league except BlueGreen. Instead Medina Sod rode Jaxon Smith-Njigba's 35.36 to a 127.70–127.28 escape and stays unbeaten. Last week Al ended a 21-month losing streak. This week he found a new way to lose, and it may be the cruelest one yet.

Brian Kulp and the Bears, Part III

Previously: Kulp renamed his team CadillacKulpDaBears after Chicago hung 59 on Carolina, then the Bears scored three points. This week the rebrand is gone. CadillacKulpDeville is back on the marquee after one week, and Luther Burden, Colston Loveland and Rome Odunze all went to the bench. Caleb Williams was on IR with the hamstring, Tyson Bagent was stuck in concussion protocol, and Chicago started Case Keenum, its third-string quarterback, on Monday night against an undefeated Philadelphia team. Keenum threw a touchdown to Burden on the opening drive, and the Bears won 27–7.

Kulp's benched Bears scored 34.30: Burden 19.80, Odunze 7.40, Loveland 7.10. He lost to TJ by 5.14. Jahmyr Gibbs went for 41.40, the best game in the league, and Joe Burrow, whom Kulp grabbed off Al's scrap pile at 12:24 a.m. Saturday by Yahoo's clock, threw three touchdowns for 23.58. It still wasn't enough, because Justin Jefferson hurt his ankle (5.20), Tetairoa McMillan caught two balls (3.70), and Terrance Ferguson, picked up at 9:32 Sunday morning, re-aggravated his ankle and scored 1.90. The best lineup on Kulp's roster was 168.68, and it started all three Bears. The Bears aren't just in Kulp's head anymore. They're paying rent.

Mohn's Closing Thought

This was the week the bench won. Three teams lost with a winning lineup sitting right there, and Al lost the cruelest way possible: his quarterback threw two touchdowns to his tight end, and his tight end was watching with him from the couch. Kulp tried to break up with the Bears and they won without him. Kevin put the old name back on and scored 167. Changing names is working for exactly one person in this league.
