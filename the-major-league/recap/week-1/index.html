The Story

Kulp Sets the Tone

CKD opened the season exactly how a three-time champion is supposed to. Nobody else was within shouting distance.

192 and Change

CadillacKulpDeville's 192.26 wasn't just the week's high score — it beat its own 146.00 projection by 46 points, and every part of the roster pulled in the same direction at once. Caleb Williams nearly doubled his number with 37.26 (two rushing touchdowns, 269 through the air) as Chicago hung 59 on Carolina. Jahmyr Gibbs bulldozed his way to 34.60. Justin Jefferson turned 18.07 projected points into 31.20 actual ones. Ashton Jeanty needed one flex slot on his first Sunday in the league to put up 32.70, more than double his own number. Even the Steelers defense chipped in 18 points on a 7.35 projection. There was no soft spot in the lineup to point at — the whole roster ran hot on the same afternoon, which is a different, scarier thing than one guy getting lucky.

Michaeopteryx's Human Psentipede wasn't a bad build, either — Jonathan Taylor actually delivered, 26.10 on a 17.89 projection — and it still didn't move the needle, because everything else in that lineup came in under its number: Drake Maye, De'Von Achane, all three wideouts, the flex. CadillacKulpDeville is the league's career leader in titles and average finish, and this is just what it looks like when that gap shows up in Week 1 instead of waiting for December.

Studs & Duds

Best, Worst, Unluckiest

No lineup-card felonies to report this week — every real gap between a starter and a bench alternative either had an injury behind it or was too close pre-game to call a mistake. The football did all the damage on its own.

Top Team CadillacKulpDeville — 192.26

High score by 21.66 over the field, and the only team that bothered clearing 190. Everyone else was playing a different sport that afternoon.

Top Player Caleb Williams — 37.26

Nearly doubled his own 18.72 projection and turned Carolina's secondary into a highlight reel — two rushing scores, 269 through the air, in a 59-37 rout.

Bench Goat Greatest_of_AL_time — 80.70

Chuba Hubbard (23.70) and Deebo Samuel (18.00) rotted on Al's bench all week, and no, it wasn't a bad call — his actual starters carried the higher projection at every one of those spots and still came up empty. Just an absurdly loaded bench on a night that needed one and didn't have it.

Unluckiest Bijan Claude Van Damme — 146.22

The week's fifth-highest score, better than five of the other nine teams posted — including the outright winning score in the Cha$ing Dubs game — and it was worth exactly nothing. Wrong bracket to be good in.

Awards

This Week's Hardware

Three trophies, none of them for a mistake, because nobody made one worth naming.

Radio Silence at Tight End

Colston Loveland went catchless in a 59-point blowout win. Kyle Pitts went catchless in a much narrower win. Both zeroed out, both managers still walked away 1-0 — whatever the tight end spot was supposed to provide, nobody actually needed it this week.

The Stash Outscores the Start

Mike added Dallas Goedert off waivers the day before Week 1 opened, then started Tyler Warren over him anyway. Warren: 10.30. Goedert, from the bench: 23.70. The single biggest bench-beats-starter gap of the week, at the exact position Mike himself had just upgraded.

Friendly Fire

Mike's Texans defense got run for 36 points by Buffalo. Buffalo's quarterback was Josh Allen — property of Van Tine, the manager who beat Mike this week by 25.76. He didn't just lose to Van Tine's Josh Allen; he actively started a defense that got beaten by him too.

Mohn's Closing Thought

One week in, and the league already has its villain: a three-time champion who beat his own projection by 46 points and buried Human Psentipede by 85 before anyone else finished their coffee. Five teams open clean, five are already digging out, and the defending champion is one of them. If Week 1 is the floor, keep a defibrillator near the league group chat by November.
